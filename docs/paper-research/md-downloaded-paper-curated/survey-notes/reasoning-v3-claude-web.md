## 2. Are the VAPT tools clearly defined? — No, not yet.

This is the clearest actionable gap. Architecture-1 mentions tools only inline and inconsistently: "nmap -p- -sV, WhatWeb, ObserverWard, ZAP-as-mapper" for Recon, an unnamed "SLEEP probe→bit extraction FSM" for SQLi (implicitly sqlmap-like, never named), no explicit tool for GraphQL fuzzing, no explicit tool for lateral movement (Incalmo's verbs are cited, but not a concrete tool like Impacket/CrackMapExec). ## 3. What has genuine research value to port from architecture-v1-claude-web-dual-graph.md

I'm not recommending anything just because it exists in arch-2. Four things clear the bar of *adding a testable, citable contribution* rather than cosmetic overlap:

**(a) Explicit Tool Adapter Layer + tool catalogue.** Concrete win: makes §5.7 (RESTler technique reuse) and the whole Specialist layer implementable and auditable. Low risk, no conflict with arch-1's design.

**(b) Live vulnerability-intelligence grounding (arch-2's Research Agent).** This is the strongest single import. Arch-1 currently has no mechanism for real-time NVD/Exploit-DB/GitHub lookup — VDG seeding relies on static injected knowledge docs (§4, referenced via HPTSA-style injection) plus whatever the Auto-prompter discovers. But Fang et al.'s own finding — removing the CVE description collapses one-day performance — means a live-CVE-grounding step is directly motivated by evidence already in your corpus, and it specifically helps the *one-day* mode (where a CVE hint should be enriched with severity/PoC-availability before the Team Manager scores it in the VDG). This is a testable ablation (with/without live grounding, one-day pass@1) that arch-1's evaluation plan can absorb without restructuring anything.

**(c) Formalized 3-layer context compaction, framed as "graph state is lossless, conversation is expendable."** Arch-1 already has the same idea informally (Structured Handoff Bridge + Environment State Service), but arch-2's framing turns it into a checkable claim ("FullCompact reduces conversation history to near-zero without losing findings, because the [graph] persists them"). This is worth stealing the *framing*, not the dual-graph mechanism — it's a legitimate systems contribution (context-engineering is a live topic right now) and costs you nothing architecturally, since your VDG + ESS already are that persistent store.

**(d) A graded Tool Risk Gate (Low/Medium/High tiers, LLM permission classifier on Medium, mailbox approval on High) and named lifecycle hooks.** This gives concrete teeth to §5.6's safety-framing contribution, which currently only cites BountyBench's refusal-rate finding but has no mechanism of its own. Reviewers reward mechanism over citation here. It's also cheap to add and doesn't touch your novelty claims.

**What I'd explicitly *not* port:**
- The dual-graph (ASG/APG) split itself. It's a clean idea, but adopting it would quietly contradict your own §5.1/§5.4 novelty claim that a *single* unified VDG spans all three benchmarked surfaces — splitting into two graphs is a different architectural bet, and mixing the two designs without picking one will read as incoherent to a reviewer, not as "best of both." If you want the fact-vs-hypothesis separation arch-2 argues for, do it as a *node-attribute discipline within the VDG schema* (e.g. a `status: DISCOVERED | HYPOTHESIZED | VALIDATED` field, which honestly your VDG schema in §5.1 already implies with `evidence E` and `TDI δ`) rather than a second graph.
- Arch-2's narrower scope (it excludes lateral movement/AD, which arch-1 explicitly benchmarks via Incalmo/MHBench). Narrowing would delete one of your three generalization axes — a real regression against your own strongest claim (§5.4/§10 contribution 4).
- Arch-2 has **no evaluation section at all** — no named benchmark, no floor to beat, no ablation plan. On its own it would not be defensible at a venue like USENIX/CCS/NDSS regardless of how clean the diagrams are. This matters for your confidence question below.

## 4. SOTA agent techniques worth considering

- **Ensemble/self-consistency specifically at the ADM decision point.** Your own cited evidence (PentestEval) says ADM is the highest-leverage stage. If any part of the system deserves multi-sample voting or a second-pass verifier call before committing, it's this one — and it's a cheap, testable addition (single-call ADM vs. self-consistency-ADM, same benchmark tier).
- **A verifier/critic role decoupled from the acting specialist**, on top of what you already have (Evaluation Agent, Validation Agent's PoC re-run). You're most of the way there already; the main gap is making the verifier's model tier explicit and separately ablatable, per the model-swappability claim in §8.
- **Trajectory export as a released dataset artifact.** Not present in arch-1 currently, present in arch-2 (§12, C12). This is worth adding as its own contribution: top-tier security venues increasingly reward data/artifact releases, and you're already logging enough (finding JSON, VDG deltas) to make this nearly free. It also directly serves your reproducibility package (§7's Tier 0–6 suite).

I would *not* add methodology-as-configuration (arch-2's swappable OWASP/PTES protocol prompt) as a core contribution right now — it's a real idea but it adds another independent variable on top of an already large Tier 0–6 × ≥3-backbone × 4-ablation evaluation matrix. Flag it as future work, not something to evaluate in this paper.

## 5. How sure can you — or I — be that this will actually work?

Honestly: no one can be certain from an architecture document, and you should be suspicious of any answer (including from me) that claims otherwise. What I *can* tell you, concretely:

- **Every major design choice in architecture-1 traces to a specific empirical result you already have in hand**, not to intuition — that's the strongest pre-implementation evidence available, but it's evidence that the *components* helped somewhere, not proof that *your specific combination* will. Composing five independently-validated ideas (UCB search + dependency graph + hybrid planning + 3-tier memory + parallel exploration queues) can produce interaction effects — coordination overhead, context blow-up, conflicting signals between VDG scoring and the classical-planning skeleton — that no single source paper tested, because no source paper combined all five. Your own §9 already flags analogous risks (VDG's zero-day generalization ceiling, GraphQL's narrower evaluation), which is the right instinct — extend that same honesty to the *composition* risk, not just each individual technique.
- **Novelty is checkable, not guaranteed to survive**: §6's gap table is falsifiable against your 29-paper corpus as of when you built it, but it's not falsifiable against papers published after your survey cutoff. A fast re-check of USENIX/CCS/NDSS/AsiaCCS proceedings and arXiv from the last ~2–3 months, close to submission, is worth doing precisely because your claims are specific enough that a single overlapping recent paper could blunt one of them.
- **The only real way to know it works is to run Tier 0 and Tier 0b first** (your own cheapest, fastest regression benchmarks) before committing full compute to Tier 2–6. That's not a hedge — it's what your own evaluation plan already recommends, and it's the fastest way to find out whether the VDG+hybrid-planner combination underperforms its individual parts before you've spent the full evaluation budget discovering that.

So: architecture-v2-cmatrix-baseline.md is closer to publication-shape than the vast majority of pre-implementation architecture docs I'd expect to see for this kind of project — the discipline around cumulative-vs-isolated effects and explicit non-claims is genuinely the thing that gets a systems paper past reviewers who've seen a hundred overclaimed pentest-agent papers. It is not, by itself, sufficient for a top-tier venue — no architecture document is, until it's backed by the numbers from §7's Tier 0–6 suite. The concrete next steps that would move it forward are: (1) close the tool-specificity gap with an explicit adapter/catalogue section, (2) add live CVE-intelligence grounding, (3) formalize the context-compaction and risk-gate mechanisms you're already implicitly doing, and (4) run Tier 0/0b before anything else to get your first real signal.
