\section{Overview}
\label{sec_overview}

In the evolving landscape of cybersecurity, organizations face a continuous threat from sophisticated attacks, necessitating the development of advanced threat detection and mitigation mechanisms. Traditional Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems provide real-time monitoring, detection, and incident response capabilities, but often lack explainability and contextual awareness when dealing with large-scale security events. Meanwhile, recent advancements in Generative AI (GenAI) have introduced powerful models that can enhance threat analysis through context-aware intelligence.

This research introduces DataLex, a novel SIEM-GenAI integrated system designed to enhance cybersecurity monitoring and response by combining traditional SIEM functionalities with Generative AI capabilities. The system employs Elasticsearch, Wazuh-dashboard, Filebeat, and Suricata to collect, process, and visualize security data, while LLAMA 3.0, FAISS, and LangChain enable AI-driven threat analysis and user interaction via a conversational assistant.

The primary objective of this work is to bridge the gap between rule-based threat detection and AI-driven contextual analysis, ensuring privacy, efficiency, and explainability. The proposed system model leverages Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) techniques for intelligent log analysis, improving the precision of responses while maintaining an offline and privacy-preserving approach.

This chapter outlines the system architecture, core components, and methodological approach used to develop DataLex. It also discusses key assumptions, experimental design, and technical implementations that guide the integration of SIEM with AI-driven threat intelligence.
