\section{Summary}
\label{sec_chapter2_summary}

The eleven papers reviewed here span the main approaches to autonomous, LLM-driven penetration testing developed through 2025. Early single-agent work (Fang et al., PentestGPT) established the basic capability and its limits; HPTSA and VulnBot introduced multi-agent orchestration; CHECKMATE brought explicit prerequisite modeling; Incalmo and PrediQL extended the problem to multi-host networks and GraphQL APIs respectively. CVE-Bench and PentestEval supply the quantitative benchmarks that make the field's current limitations measurable rather than merely asserted. Across this literature, the comparative analysis surfaces five structural patterns: exploration-capable systems dispatch vulnerabilities without a prerequisite model; dependency-modeling systems require pre-enumerated or schema-provided weakness sets rather than operating on dynamically discovered output; no system retains information across separate engagements; the two benchmark studies converge on the same compound failure from opposite measurement angles; and every autonomous system in the reviewed set has been evaluated on a single attack-surface family.

Section~\ref{sec_research_gap} translates these patterns into a precise three-dimensional gap: no reviewed system combines open-ended attack-surface discovery with a dynamically constructed prerequisite-aware dependency model and evaluation across more than one independently benchmarked surface family. Chapter~\ref{chapter_proposedMethod} introduces the RedGrid architecture --- the four-layer orchestration hierarchy, Dual-Layer World Model, and VDG --- as a first design response to this gap, with the specification subject to refinement as implementation proceeds.
