\section{Research Gap}
\label{sec_research_gap}

The literature surveyed in this chapter suggests, tentatively, that autonomous VAPT systems have so far been optimized for one of two capabilities at a time, rather than both together. On one side, systems built for broad exploration of an unfamiliar attack surface -- HPTSA, MAPTA, and similar team-dispatch architectures -- do not appear to model prerequisite relationships between candidate weaknesses explicitly; they search widely but without a formal notion of what must be established before what. On the other side, systems that do reason explicitly about such dependencies, including CHECKMATE's classical-planning layer and PentestEval's ground-truth attack decision-making configuration, are evaluated on scenarios where the relevant weaknesses have already been enumerated in advance, and it is not yet clear from the surveyed papers how well these approaches would scale to open-ended discovery on a wide, previously unseen surface.

This split is echoed in how the two most detailed benchmarks in the corpus report their results. CVE-Bench's own failure analysis attributes the majority of unsuccessful attempts on its hardest real-world CVEs to insufficient exploration rather than to reasoning quality, while PentestEval's stage-level breakdown finds that the attack-decision-making stage -- reasoning about which weakness to pursue next given what has already been discovered -- is consistently the weakest stage across the systems it evaluates. Taken together, these two findings point at what looks, at this preliminary stage of the review, like two sides of the same underlying gap: no system surveyed so far appears to combine broad, open-ended exploration with an explicit, dynamically-constructed model of dependencies between discovered weaknesses.

A secondary observation, of lower priority but still worth noting, is that cross-session memory and evaluation across more than one attack-surface family (web, GraphQL, multi-host) are both rare in the surveyed corpus, which may also represent open areas worth further reading.

It should be stressed that this is an early-stage reading of the literature. The gap described above is offered as a working hypothesis to guide the remainder of the review and the early planning of the thesis, rather than as a finalized problem statement or a commitment to a specific architectural solution -- both of which will be developed and justified more carefully as the work progresses over the coming months.
