\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:intro_objectives}

Guided by the two gaps identified in Section~\ref{sec:intro_problem}, we have set out the following working objectives for this thesis. These reflect the research questions we are currently investigating, not a checklist of work already completed. The exact scope and design of each objective is expected to be refined as implementation and early experiments provide feedback that the literature review alone cannot.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To investigate whether representing candidate vulnerabilities as a graph with explicit prerequisite relationships --- rather than as an unordered list --- can meaningfully improve how an autonomous agent chooses what to explore next. This includes formalizing such a structure (which we are currently calling a \textbf{Vulnerability Dependency Graph, or VDG}) to a level of precision sufficient for implementation and empirical testing.
    \item To examine ways of separating what an agent has \emph{confirmed} about a target from what it is merely \emph{hypothesising}, so that discovery quality and planning quality can eventually be evaluated somewhat independently of each other.
    \item To determine an appropriate multi-layer orchestration structure for the overall agent --- drawing on the structural patterns that recur across the strongest-performing systems in the reviewed literature --- and to develop this structure to the point where it is implementable.
    \item To consider whether allowing an agent to retain and reuse strategies across multiple engagements offers a genuine advantage, or whether it primarily introduces risk (for example, a strategy that succeeded against one version of a technology being harmful against a different version).
    \item To identify which existing, published benchmarks are appropriate for evaluating this class of system, and to begin drafting an evaluation plan around them rather than constructing new benchmarks independently.
\end{enumerate}
