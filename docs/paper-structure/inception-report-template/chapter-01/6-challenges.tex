\section{Challenges}
\label{sec:intro_challenges}

Several challenges surfaced repeatedly while we were reading the literature, and we expect them to shape our work over the next six months regardless of which specific design choices we ultimately make. We are listing them here, at an early stage, rather than waiting to encounter them during implementation:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Inferring dependencies without ground truth.} Any prerequisite structure our agent builds on its own will be inferred by an LLM rather than annotated by an expert, and we expect this to be the single riskiest part of whatever we build. Before relying on such a structure for any evaluation, we plan to first run a small pilot study against PentestEval's expert-annotated dependencies to get a sense of how accurate our inference actually is.
    \item \textbf{Sandbox results may not transfer to the real world.} \citet{fang2024hackwebsites} found only 1 exploitable XSS in 50 real-world candidate sites (2\%), against 73.3\% in a matched sandbox. We intend to keep this gap in mind throughout and to avoid presenting sandbox numbers as if they were real-world numbers.
    \item \textbf{Some benchmarks are small.} PrediQL's GraphQL suite, for instance, covers only 6 APIs --- real and standardised, but far smaller than benchmarks like CVE-Bench. Any conclusion drawn from it will need to be stated as exploratory rather than definitive.
    \item \textbf{Cost is a moving target.} Whatever cost-per-exploit figures we eventually report will be tied to a specific model and pricing at a specific point in time, and we plan to state that explicitly rather than presenting cost as a fixed property of the approach.
    \item \textbf{Reused strategies could backfire.} A strategy that worked against one version of a piece of software could plausibly be actively harmful against a different version. We have not found this risk addressed in the papers we reviewed, which is one reason we consider it worth attention from the start.
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity to configuration choices.} If our approach depends on several tunable parameters, we would want to check how sensitive results are to those choices rather than reporting numbers from a single, possibly fortunate, configuration.
    \item \textbf{Being honest about what generalises.} If we do end up testing across more than one attack surface, we want to be careful about the difference between ``a shared design'' and ``one literally unmodified system'', since we expect surface-specific components to be necessary either way.
\end{enumerate}
