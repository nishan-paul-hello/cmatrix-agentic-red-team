\section{Expected Contributions}
\label{sec:intro_contributions}

The items below correspond to the objectives in Section~\ref{sec:intro_objectives}, restated here as design commitments and empirical hypotheses rather than accomplished results. Each will be evaluated against the benchmarks described in Section~\ref{sec:intro_scope}; the framing will be adjusted as implementation proceeds.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{A proposed system architecture.} The primary architectural contribution is a four-layer orchestration hierarchy --- Mission Planner, Team Manager, Specialist Agents, and Execution/Validation Layer --- combined with a Dual-Layer World Model that separates empirically confirmed target state (Evidence Layer) from inferred but unconfirmed hypotheses (Analysis Layer, hosting the VDG). This design synthesises structural patterns that recur independently across the strongest-performing systems in the reviewed literature \citep{zhu2024hptsa,kong2025vulnbot,singer2025incalmo} and addresses the mismatch identified in Sections~\ref{sec:intro_problem} and~\ref{sec:intro_objectives}: no reviewed system combines both layers simultaneously. Whether this architecture delivers a performance improvement over flat-dispatch baselines is an empirical question; the architecture itself, together with its formal specification, is a contribution independent of that outcome.

	\item \textbf{Dependency-aware exploration via the VDG.} The primary empirical hypothesis is that a \textbf{Vulnerability Dependency Graph (VDG)} --- a dynamically constructed directed graph in which edges encode prerequisite relationships between candidate weaknesses, and node selection is governed by a UCB policy --- improves end-to-end exploitation success relative to flat-dispatch baselines. The quantitative opportunity is bounded: the current best end-to-end baseline without ground truth is 0.31 (PentestEval SMP); the upper bound with perfect attack-decision-making is 0.67 (PentestEval GT-ADM ablation). The target is an improvement somewhere within this range, measured on CVE-Bench \citep{zhu2025cvebench} and PentestEval \citep{yang2025pentesteval}. No position on the expected magnitude of improvement is available with confidence at this stage.

	\item \textbf{Cross-mission memory and selective strategy reuse.} A secondary hypothesis is that a \textbf{Cross-Mission Memory} module --- retaining and selectively replaying successful attack strategies from prior engagements, keyed by target technology fingerprint --- produces a measurable benefit on targets sharing underlying technology, without inducing the negative-transfer risk discussed in Section~\ref{sec:intro_challenges}. Whether this becomes a core research contribution or supporting infrastructure will be determined by what early design work reveals.

	\item \textbf{Multi-surface evaluation with separated per-surface reporting.} Independently of whether the above hypotheses hold, this thesis intends to evaluate the proposed system against at least two independently published benchmarks spanning distinct attack-surface families --- CVE-Bench and PentestEval (web applications), PrediQL (GraphQL APIs) \citep{liu2025prediql}, and Incalmo MHBench (multi-host Active Directory environments) \citep{singer2025incalmo} --- with standardised oracles and per-surface reporting. Every reviewed system undergoes evaluation on a single attack-surface family, making it impossible to distinguish architectural gains from surface-specific tuning effects. Multi-surface evaluation with separated reporting is therefore a methodological contribution in itself, regardless of the numeric outcome.
\end{itemize}

The engineering infrastructure that enables the above --- structured inter-agent communication protocols, retry-and-diagnose validation loops, deterministic tool-call logging, and benchmark harness integration --- is a necessary precondition for reproducible evaluation rather than an independent research claim. It is listed here for completeness and will be documented in detail in Chapter~\ref{chapter_implementations}.
